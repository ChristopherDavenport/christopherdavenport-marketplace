# Chart of Accounts — FI Structure Mapped to Call Report

## Overview

The chart of accounts (COA) is the **catalog of every GL account** an institution maintains, organized into a hierarchy that supports both financial reporting and regulatory reporting. For a US depository institution, the COA is typically designed to **roll up directly to Call Report line items** so that regulatory reports can be generated by aggregation rather than by mapping logic.

This reference documents a representative FI COA aligned to the FFIEC Call Report (forms 031, 041, 051) and identifies which accounts are **control accounts** for sub-ledgers (see [sub-ledgers.md](sub-ledgers.md)).

## Citation

- **Call Report forms and instructions**: FFIEC 031 (banks with foreign offices), 041 (domestic only), 051 (small institution, ≤ $5B and qualifying)
- **Authoritative source**: `https://www.ffiec.gov/ffiec_report_forms.htm`
- **Schedules referenced below**: RC (balance sheet), RI (income statement), RC-C (loans), RC-E (deposits), RC-K (averages), RC-N (past-due/nonaccrual), RC-R (regulatory capital)

## Design Principles

1. **One COA, multiple report rollups** — the same COA should generate Call Report, GAAP financial statements, and management reporting through aggregation rules
2. **Control accounts for everything with a sub-ledger** — never let detail accumulate directly in the GL
3. **Contra-account discipline** — ALLL, accumulated depreciation, deferred fees: keep contra accounts adjacent to their parents in the COA
4. **Segregate by Call Report line** — if two products have different Call Report treatment, they need different GL accounts (or segments)
5. **Don't repurpose accounts** — once an account is used and reported, it cannot be repurposed without restating prior periods
6. **Approval for new accounts** — new GL accounts should require controller approval to prevent COA proliferation
