# Ledgers — The General Ledger

## Overview

The general ledger (GL) is the **system of record for an institution's financial position and results**. It is organized by account (one record per chart-of-accounts entry), where each account's balance is the running sum of all journal entries posted to it. The GL is the source for every financial report: balance sheet, income statement, cash-flow statement, regulatory Call Report, and management reporting.

Where journals are **chronological** (transactions in time order), the ledger is **categorical** (transactions grouped by account). The journal answers "what happened on May 4th?"; the ledger answers "what is the balance of Loans — Commercial Real Estate?"

## Citation

Foundational accounting concept; not codified. Presentation of resulting financial statements is governed by ASC 205 (Presentation of Financial Statements), ASC 210 (Balance Sheet), ASC 220 (Income Statement and Comprehensive Income), and ASC 942 for FIs.

## Structure & Key Sections

### Account Hierarchy

The GL is a tree, organized by the chart of accounts (see [chart-of-accounts.md](chart-of-accounts.md)). Top-level structure for an FI:

```
1xxxxx  Assets
  10xxxx   Cash and Due From Banks
  11xxxx   Investment Securities
  13xxxx   Loans and Leases
  14xxxx   Allowance for Credit Losses (contra)
  16xxxx   Premises and Equipment
  17xxxx   Other Real Estate Owned
  18xxxx   Other Assets
2xxxxx  Liabilities
  20xxxx   Deposits
  24xxxx   Borrowings
  28xxxx   Other Liabilities
3xxxxx  Equity
  30xxxx   Common Stock
  35xxxx   Retained Earnings
  38xxxx   AOCI
4xxxxx  Interest Income
5xxxxx  Interest Expense
6xxxxx  Provision for Credit Losses
7xxxxx  Non-Interest Income
8xxxxx  Non-Interest Expense
```

Numbering is institution-specific; the example above is conventional. The actual COA aligns to Call Report line items so regulatory reporting can be generated by aggregation rather than mapping. See [chart-of-accounts.md](chart-of-accounts.md).

### GL Account Number Segmentation

Modern core-banking GLs use **segmented account numbers** to support multi-dimensional reporting without proliferating accounts:

```
{Branch} - {Cost Center} - {Account} - {Product} - {Currency}
   001   -    1100      -  130100   -   CRE     -   USD
```

Segments enable reporting at multiple grains (e.g., total CRE loans across all branches, vs. CRE loans at branch 001). Segment definitions vary by institution and core platform.

### Posting

A posting is the act of moving an amount from a journal entry to the affected ledger account. In modern systems posting is automatic and synchronous with transaction entry. Conceptually:

```
Journal Entry (chronological)        →    Ledger Accounts (categorical)
2026-05-04                                Loans — CRE          : Bal 12,500,000 + 500,000 = 13,000,000
  Dr Loans — CRE       500,000            Cash — Fed account   : Bal  8,200,000 - 500,000 =  7,700,000
  Cr Cash — Fed        500,000
```

Posting rules:
- One journal entry can post to many GL accounts (one per debit/credit line)
- Posted balance updates are atomic — partial posting is not permitted
- A posted entry cannot be deleted; it can only be reversed by an offsetting entry (audit trail preservation)

### T-Accounts

A T-account is the **visual representation of a single ledger account**:

```
        Loans — Commercial Real Estate
        Debits          |    Credits
   ─────────────────────┼──────────────────
   Beg bal:  12,500,000 |
        +      500,000  |   ─    25,000   (principal payment)
        +      300,000  |   ─    50,000   (charge-off)
   ─────────────────────┼──────────────────
   End bal:  13,225,000 |
```

T-accounts are a teaching/analysis tool, not a system structure — but they are how accountants reason about a single account's activity during a period.

### Trial Balance

A trial balance is a **listing of all GL account balances at a point in time**, with debits in one column and credits in another. The total of debits must equal the total of credits — this is the basic proof that double-entry has been preserved.

Three variants:

| Variant | When Run | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Unadjusted trial balance | Before period-end adjusting entries | Starting point for close |
| Adjusted trial balance | After adjusting entries (accruals, deferrals, FV marks, provisions) | Source for financial statements |
| Post-closing trial balance | After closing entries | Confirms only permanent accounts carry balances |

**A balanced trial balance proves the GL is internally consistent. It does NOT prove correctness** — entries can balance and still be posted to the wrong accounts. That's why sub-ledger reconciliation (see [sub-ledgers.md](sub-ledgers.md)) and analytical review are required.

### Control Accounts

A **control account** is a GL account whose balance equals the sum of detail held in a corresponding sub-ledger. The GL holds a single line; the sub-ledger holds the detail (one record per loan, deposit, customer, etc.).

Common FI control accounts:

| GL Control Account | Sub-Ledger |
|---|---|
| Loans — Commercial | Loan servicing system, by loan number |
| Loans — Consumer | Loan servicing system, by loan number |
| Deposits — Demand | DDA system, by account number |
| Deposits — Savings | Savings system, by account number |
| Deposits — Time (CDs) | CD system, by account number |
| Investment Securities — AFS | Investment portfolio system, by CUSIP |
| Accrued Interest Receivable | Loan/securities systems, by instrument |
| Accrued Interest Payable | Deposit system, by account |
| Premises and Equipment | Fixed asset sub-ledger, by asset ID |

Reconciliation (sub-to-GL tieout) is a daily or monthly control. See [sub-ledgers.md](sub-ledgers.md).

### Period Close

End-of-period (typically month-end and year-end) close sequence in an FI:

1. **Cutoff** — all transactions for the period are posted; subsequent transactions held for next period
2. **Sub-ledger reconciliation** — every control account tied to its sub-ledger; breaks investigated and resolved
3. **Accruals booked** — interest accruals, expense accruals, fee accruals, bonus accruals
4. **Valuation entries** — AFS fair value marks, CECL provision, OREO impairment, deferred tax true-up
5. **Adjusted trial balance** generated and reviewed
6. **Financial statements** generated (balance sheet, income statement, comprehensive income)
7. **Call Report data** extracted (FFIEC 031/041/051) and validated
8. **Closing entries** booked at fiscal year-end (close P&L to retained earnings)
9. **Post-closing trial balance** confirms permanent accounts only
10. **Books locked** — period cannot be reopened without controller approval

### GL Modifications and the Audit Trail

Once posted, GL entries are immutable. Corrections are made via reversing or adjusting entries (see [journals.md](journals.md)). The audit trail must show:
- Original entry (date, user, source system, reference)
- Any reversing/correcting entries linked to it
- Posted-to balances after each entry
- Approver identity for manual entries

This is required for FDICIA Part 363 (insured depositories ≥ $1B), SOX 404 (public companies), and SSAE 18 SOC reports for service organizations.
